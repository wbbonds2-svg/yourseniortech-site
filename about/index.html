About Senior Tech Foundation

A Washington nonprofit helping older adults use technology confidently, safely, and independently, through patient support from trained student mentors.

What is Senior Tech Foundation? Senior Tech Foundation is a Washington 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN 41-3502697) serving older adults in King and Pierce Counties. Trained high school and college mentors provide free one-on-one tech help, Mobile Tech Clinics, and digital safety workshops.

Our mission

Senior Tech Foundation exists to make sure older adults are not left behind in an increasingly digital world. We help seniors use technology confidently, safely, and independently through patient support, practical education, inclusive access, and intergenerational connection.

Why this work matters

As technology keeps changing, many seniors are being left behind. Without access to patient, trustworthy help, older adults face a higher risk of scams, misinformation, and social isolation. This gap isn’t only about tech skills. It affects independence, safety, and quality of life.

The Foundation started because so many older adults were struggling to use digital tools for communication, healthcare, and safety, and that lack of support was leading to frustration, isolation, and vulnerability.

Our long-term vision

A future where every senior has the skills, confidence, and support to thrive in a digital world. We’re building inclusive, community-based programs that promote digital literacy, protect vulnerable people, and make sure technology empowers older adults instead of shutting them out.

Our story

What started as helping with simple tech questions revealed a deeper problem. Seniors weren’t just struggling with devices. They were worried about clicking the wrong thing, falling for scams, losing access to important accounts, or feeling embarrassed to ask for help. Too often, support was rushed, impersonal, or simply unavailable.

We saw how different things became when help was patient and personal. When someone sat down, explained things clearly, and answered questions without judgment, confidence grew. A confusing phone became manageable. A suspicious text became a teachable moment. Sometimes a single conversation prevented a scam.

Senior Tech Foundation was built around that approach. By pairing older adults with trained young mentors, we create an environment where learning feels comfortable, respectful, and human. Technology shouldn’t be something seniors fear or face alone.

Our intergenerational model

Our mentors are high school and college students trained to give patient, side-by-side support. Seniors feel comfortable asking questions, and confidence grows. Mentors develop empathy, leadership, and communication skills working with older adults.

How we keep help safe

  • Mentors are trained before working with participants
  • Mentors explain each step so participants stay in control of their own devices
  • Participants enter their own passwords; mentors don’t collect or record them

Our values

Patience and dignity

Every older adult deserves to learn technology without pressure, judgment, or embarrassment. We honor each person’s pace, experience, and independence.

Access and inclusion

Age, income, ability, or prior experience should never decide who benefits from technology. We work to reduce digital exclusion for older adults of all backgrounds.

Safety and protection

Digital safety is part of independence. We help seniors recognize scams, protect their privacy, and use technology with confidence rather than fear.

Clarity and practical education

Technology should be useful, not overwhelming. We focus on clear explanations and real-world skills that support everyday life.

Intergenerational connection

Technology can bring generations together. Students and young adults supporting seniors builds mutual respect and stronger communities.

Community and trust

We build long-term relationships by showing up consistently, listening, and delivering support communities can depend on.
